1Can you trust what an LLM reads?

Not its confidence. Check every answer with code.

Four checks on every model answer
Sanitizer: injection patterns in EN/KO/JA/ZH, field whitelist
Arithmetic: subtotal + tax + tip + discount = total
Date plausibility
Two-reader agreement: the total and date must appear in the OCR text

Fig A. Where 300 scans end up under each policy, accepted-answer accuracy and USD per 1,000.

The harness: 96.9% correct with 56% of receipts sent to the cloud and 4.3% to people, $0.37 per 1,000 (all cloud: 97.3%, $0.54). The strong model adds 0.7 points for 2.7× the price: escalate to people, not to a bigger model.

Fig B. Escalation curve: share sent to the cloud vs accuracy as the threshold moves; ★ = a router that knows the answers.

A perfect router needs only 27% cloud for 98.7%; the rule signal needs 56% for 95.7%. The bottleneck is the escalation signal, not the model.

A learned signal (logistic regression, 14 features, out-of-fold AUC 0.89) did no better at the same cloud share, so the two rules stayed.

3What if the data can't leave?

Run local-only: same checks, more human review.

Cloud harnessLocal only Scans96.9% correct93.4% correct 4.3% to people · $0.37/1k19% to people · $0 · 27 s Photos88.5% correct89.1% correct 21.5% to people31.5% to people

On phone photos the two modes match on accuracy and differ only in review load; on scans local only gives up 3.4 points. Keeping data in costs mostly people's time.

In cloud mode the image leaves the machine at one step only; every check and routing decision runs locally.

Check rules are domain assets: a rule built from scan errors caught 10 of 16 there with no false alarms, but 1 receipt on held-out photos, with 1 false alarm.
